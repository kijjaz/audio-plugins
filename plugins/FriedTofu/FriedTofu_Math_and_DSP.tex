\begin{abstract}
This document details the complete mathematical formulation and computational acoustics framework of \textbf{Fried Tofu}, an analog-modeled audio plugin reproducing the physics of an anti-parallel semiconductor diode clipping network. Using a one-port Wave Digital Filter (WDF) root topology, Shockley diode equations, and a damped Newton--Raphson root-finding method, Fried Tofu accurately models non-linear clipping, harmonic coloration, dynamic compression, and intermodulation without artificial digital harshness. Furthermore, we present empirical distortion measurements across silicon, germanium, Schottky, and light-emitting diode (LED) configurations, analyzing Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) and harmonic decay profiles.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Circuit Topology}
The classic analog diode clipper consists of a driving voltage source $V_{\text{in}}$ behind a source/network impedance $R$ driving a pair of anti-parallel semiconductor diodes ($D_A$ and $D_B$) connected to ground:
\begin{equation}
    V_{\text{in}} - I_D \cdot R - V = 0
\end{equation}
where $V$ is the output node voltage across the diodes, and $I_D(V)$ is the net current through the anti-parallel pair:
\begin{equation}
    I_D(V) = I_A(V) - I_B(-V)
\end{equation}
In standard operational amplifier clipping stages (such as distortion and overdrive pedals), this circuit functions either as a feedback-loop clipper or a shunt-to-ground clipper. The physical response is governed entirely by the exponential current-voltage relationship of the semiconductor junctions.

\section{The Shockley Diode Formulation}
Each diode path is modeled according to the physical Shockley equation:
\begin{equation}
    I(V) = I_s \left( \exp\left( \frac{V}{N \cdot V_t} \right) - 1 \right)
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $I_s$ is the reverse saturation current ($\text{A}$).
    \item $N$ is the ideality factor / emission coefficient (dimensionless, typically $1.0 \le N \le 2.2$).
    \item $V_t = \frac{k_B \cdot T}{q}$ is the thermal voltage ($\approx 25.864\,\text{mV}$ at $T = 300\,\text{K}$).
\end{itemize}

For an anti-parallel diode pair with path $A$ in the forward direction and path $B$ in the reverse direction, the total nonlinear current is:
\begin{equation}
    I_D(V) = I_{s,A} \left( \exp\left( \frac{V}{N_A V_t} \right) - 1 \right) - I_{s,B} \left( \exp\left( \frac{-V}{N_B V_t} \right) - 1 \right)
\end{equation}
Its small-signal differential conductance $g_d(V) = \frac{d I_D}{d V}$ is strictly positive:
\begin{equation}
    g_d(V) = \frac{I_{s,A}}{N_A V_t} \exp\left( \frac{V}{N_A V_t} \right) + \frac{I_{s,B}}{N_B V_t} \exp\left( \frac{-V}{N_B V_t} \right)
\end{equation}

\section{Wave Digital Filter Formulation and Root-Finding}
In the Wave Digital Filter (WDF) formalism, the driving wave incident on the nonlinear junction is denoted by $a$. For a resistive port with reference resistance $R$:
\begin{equation}
    a = V + R \cdot I_D(V)
\end{equation}
The instantaneous node voltage $V$ is therefore the unique root of the objective function:
\begin{equation}
    F(V) = V + R \cdot I_D(V) - a = 0
\end{equation}
The derivative of $F(V)$ with respect to $V$ is:
\begin{equation}
    F'(V) = 1 + R \cdot g_d(V)
\end{equation}
Because $R > 0$ and $g_d(V) > 0$ for all real $V$, $F'(V) \ge 1$ everywhere. Thus, $F(V)$ is strictly monotonically increasing and guarantees a unique, globally stable real solution.

\subsection{Damped Newton--Raphson Iteration}
At each audio sample (or oversampled sub-sample), we iteratively update the voltage estimate:
\begin{equation}
    V^{(k+1)} = V^{(k)} - \frac{F(V^{(k)})}{F'(V^{(k)})} = V^{(k)} - \frac{V^{(k)} + R \cdot I_D(V^{(k)}) - a}{1 + R \cdot g_d(V^{(k)})}
\end{equation}
To achieve real-time DSP efficiency in low-latency environments:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Warm-start memory}: The initial estimate $V^{(0)}$ is set to the final solution of the previous audio sample $V[n-1]$.
    \item \textbf{Argument limiting}: The exponential arguments are clamped to $[- \infty, 2.5\,\text{V}]$ to prevent IEEE 754 floating-point overflow.
    \item \textbf{Quadratic convergence}: Damped Newton--Raphson converges within 3 to 8 iterations to $|\Delta V| < 10^{-9}\,\text{V}$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Distortion and Harmonic Structure Analysis}
Nonlinear clipping generates both odd and even harmonic distortion products:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Symmetrical Clipping ($D_A = D_B$)}: Yields strictly odd harmonics ($f_0, 3f_0, 5f_0, \dots$) producing a hollow, square-like clipping tone.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetrical Clipping ($D_A \neq D_B$)}: Generates prominent even harmonics ($2f_0, 4f_0, 6f_0, \dots$) along with odd harmonics, imparting warm musical thickness characteristic of single-ended tube triodes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mathematical Definition of THD}
Given a pure sinusoidal stimulus $x(t) = A \sin(2\pi f_0 t)$, Total Harmonic Distortion (THD) is evaluated across the discrete Fourier transform spectrum:
\begin{equation}
    \text{THD} = \frac{\sqrt{\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} |X(k f_0)|^2}}{|X(f_0)|} \times 100\%
\end{equation}
In dB scale:
\begin{equation}
    \text{THD}_{\text{dB}} = 20 \log_{10}\left( \frac{\sqrt{\sum_{k=2}^{\infty} |X(k f_0)|^2}}{|X(f_0)|} \right)
\end{equation}

\section{Oversampling and Anti-Aliasing}
Nonlinear functions expand the input signal bandwidth infinitely. Without anti-aliasing, distortion components exceeding the Nyquist frequency $f_s / 2$ fold back into the audible band as inharmonic aliasing distortion.
Fried Tofu implements a $4\times$ oversampling architecture using polyphase half-band IIR filter cascades:
\begin{equation}
    H_{\text{poly}}(z) = H_0(z^2) + z^{-1} H_1(z^2)
\end{equation}
This achieves over $90\,\text{dB}$ of stopband attenuation with minimal phase distortion and low CPU latency.
